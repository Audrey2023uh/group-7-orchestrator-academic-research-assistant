Abstract 1
Autonomous multi-agent systems built on large language models exchange goals, con- 2
straints, evidence commitments, and tool-mediated actions through natural-language 3
handoffs. Across hops, semantic meaning may drift, constraints may weaken, unsupported 4
claims may propagate, and organizations may lose visibility into how decisions were pro- 5
duced. This paper develops a semantic-tracing framework that treats meaning preservation 6
as a ?rst-class measurand and operationalizes it with three LangChain ecosystem compo- 7
nents that are genuinely used in the local experimental artifact: LangChain for LLM invoca- 8
tion, prompt templates, tool interfaces, structured-output parsing, and retries; LangGraph 9
for stateful multi-agent orchestration (nodes, edges, work?ow state, routing, and human- 10
review control); and LangSmith for private tracing, span capture, metadata, and evaluation- 11
linked observability . The system separates framework orchestration, LLM reasoning, deter- 12
ministic safeguards, trace capture, semantic evaluation, and human decision. The frame- 13
work is anchored in SHADOWAI-RISK, a live academic research prototype for enterprise AI 14
governance and risk intelligence. In addition to the live prototype, this study implements a 15
separate local experimental extension with (i) a preserved deterministic baseline pathway 16
and (ii) a genuine LLM multi-agent condition (Inventory ? Evidence ? Governance ? 17
Human Review Gate) implemented with LangChain ( ChatOpenAI, ChatPromptTemplate, 18
JsonOutputParser, tool wrappers, retries), LangGraph ( StateGraph nodes/edges/state), 19
optional private LangSmith instrumentation, and a rule-based semantic evaluation layer. 20

1. Introduction 34
Organizations are currently negotiating whether and how to adopt AI under conditions 35
of cybersecurity exposure, privacy risk, hallucination, accountability gaps, unauthorized 36
AI use (Shadow AI), and potential loss of managerial control. Professional and regulatory 37
institutions?including NIST, ISACA, European institutions, ENISA, and the OECD?are 38
shaping expectations for trustworthy AI and organizational oversight [ 1? 4]. 39
At the same time, LLM-based systems are moving from single-agent assistants toward 40
multi-agent work?ows in which specialized agents plan, retrieve evidence, invoke tools, 41
critique outputs, and hand work to other agents [ 5? 8]. Classical multi-agent theory empha- 42
sized autonomy and coordinated interaction [ 9]. In LLM systems, coordination is mediated 43
by natural-language messages whose meaning is brittle [ 10,11]. 44
Observability platforms such as LangSmith capture end-to-end execution traces [ 12]. 45
AgentOps research likewise argues that lifecycle observability supports safer agent opera- 46
tion [13]. Tracing alone, however, does not answer the governance question that matters in 47
enterprise settings: whether agents preserved the meaning of what they were asked to do, 48
under which constraints, on which evidence, and with what residual risk. 49
This paper addresses that problem by de?ning semantic tracing and binding it to 50
enterprise AI risk work?ows. Figure 1 summarizes the end-to-end research logic. 51

Ji et al. survey hallucination in natural language generation [ 25? 27]. In multi-agent 102
