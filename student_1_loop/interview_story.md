# Interview Story — Infinite Loop Guardrail

**Author: Audrey Rah (Group 7) · Failure-mode package: `student_1_loop`**

During integration testing of the academic research orchestrator, I observed the coordinator re-dispatching rejected validation cycles without a hard stop. In an unguarded simulation the retry loop burned 100 iterations and about 12,000 approximate tokens before a safety hard-cap halted the run. After I implemented deterministic `round_number` tracking against the frozen shared contract, any retry storm that reaches `round_number >= 5` routes to a graceful partial-output node instead of continuing indefinitely. With the guardrail enabled, the same perpetual-rejection scenario terminates in six coordinator steps at round five, cutting token spend to about 720 units and collapsing measured latency relative to the unguarded path, while preserving a usable partial result for operators. The quantitative before/after comparison—including 11,280 tokens saved—is recorded in `metrics.md` and reproduced by `test_failure.py`, giving a concrete production control for runaway graph recursion without relying on prompt instructions alone. That code-enforced ceiling is what I would cite in a production multi-agent reliability interview.
